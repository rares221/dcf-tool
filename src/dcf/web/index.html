The discount rate (WACC), explained

In plain words: a DCF adds up the cash a company is expected to earn in the future, then shrinks it back into today's money. Money received later is worth less than the same amount today, because you could invest it elsewhere and because the future is uncertain. The discount rate is how hard it shrinks. Analysts call it WACC (weighted average cost of capital): the return that the company's investors and lenders, mixed together, demand for the risk they take.
