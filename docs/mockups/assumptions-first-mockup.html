Assumptions used

Everything below is built from these five numbers. We filled them in from Apple's own history and from market data, so you can start with them as they are. Change one at a time and watch the result bar. If you cannot say why you would change a number, leave it.

Our default from Apple's data

7.5%: Apple's average yearly revenue growth over the last 3 years (7.7%), rounded to the slider step.

Accepted values

-10% to 30% a year. Growth fades toward the long-term rate over the forecast, so it is a starting point.

Change it if…

you expect the business to speed up or slow down (new products, a maturing market, a big acquisition). For context: peers grew 9.5% last year.

Our default from Apple's data

28.5%: last year's free cash flow divided by revenue. The model then adds back interest (+0.5) and subtracts stock pay (−2.9), so it actually uses 26.1%.

Accepted values

1% to 60%. Higher margins make the business worth more, and they are the easiest number to be too hopeful about.

Change it if…

you expect costs or prices to change margins for good, not for one year. Check the track record first: 26%, 28%, 24%, 28%.

Our default partly an assumption

9.1% = 4.3% risk-free rate + Apple's beta (1.09) × a 4.5% risk premium, blended with a little debt. The 4.3% is a default: no live rate is configured.

Accepted values

5% to 16%, and always at least 1 point above long-term growth. Close to long-term growth the maths blows up, so the slider stops there.

Change it if…

you want a different required return. Higher means you want more reward for the risk, so the value is lower. This number matters most: 1 point moves the base value by about $30.

Our default our assumption

2.5%: roughly how fast the whole economy grows, applied forever after the forecast years.

Accepted values

0% to 4%, and never above the risk-free rate (4.3% here; lower for currencies with low rates).

Change it if…

rarely. Most of a company's value sits after the forecast years, so small changes here move the answer a lot. 2% to 3% is the usual range.

Our default from Apple's data

Counted as a cost. Apple pays about 2.9% of revenue in shares. That is a real cost to existing owners even though no cash leaves.

Accepted values

Count or ignore.

Change it if…

you disagree that it is a cost (some analysts add it back). Ignoring it would add about $17 a share, so it is the more flattering choice.

About the limits. The sliders only accept the values shown. That is deliberate: it keeps the maths meaningful (a discount rate too close to long-term growth would give an infinite value) and stops one typo from producing a nonsense result.
  1. Start with the defaults and read the result.
  2. Ask "what would I have to believe?" The card "What the market is already assuming" answers that.
  3. Move one slider at a time. The summary above shows what changed.
